DIY Car Checks

Simple checks you can do at home in a few minutes, with no tools. If something looks wrong, don’t drive it: call us.

Before you start your car

Tyres

Look at all four tyres and the spare for flat spots, cuts or bulges. Check the pressure with a gauge when the tyres are cold.

Under the car

Look at the ground where the car was parked. Fresh dark oil, coloured coolant or brake fluid means get it checked. Clear water drips after using the A/C are normal.

Lights

Switch on headlights, indicators, hazards and brake lights (ask someone to press the brake pedal). Replace any dead bulb.

Windshield and wipers

Look for cracks in the glass. Wipers should clear it without streaks. Top up the washer fluid.

Engine oil

Engine off, car on level ground. Pull the dipstick, wipe it, dip it again. The level should sit between MIN and MAX.

Coolant

Check the reservoir only when the engine is cold. The level should be between MIN and MAX. Never open the radiator cap on a hot engine.

Brake fluid

Look at the brake fluid reservoir. The level should sit between MIN and MAX. A level that keeps dropping needs checking.

Battery

Look at the terminals. They should be tight and free of white or green crust.

Dashboard lights

Turn the key to ON without starting. The warning lights come on, then go off after the engine starts. A light that stays on needs attention.

Summer checks

Coolant level

Heat puts extra load on the cooling system. Check the reservoir level (engine cold) before long trips.

A/C and cabin filter

If cooling is weak or the air smells, the cabin filter may be clogged or the A/C may need a check. Run the A/C regularly.

Tyre pressure

Hot roads raise tyre pressure. Check when the tyres are cold, and do not release air from hot tyres.

Battery

Heat shortens battery life. Check the terminals and get the battery voltage tested if starting feels slow.

Engine oil

Check the oil level more often on long, hot drives.

Rainy season checks

Wipers and washer

Worn wipers streak and smear in heavy rain. Replace them if they do not clear the glass cleanly.

Tyre tread

Worn tyres skid on wet roads. Look at the wear marks in the grooves, and replace tyres that are worn down.

Lights

Headlights and brake lights must all work in rain and low visibility.

Brakes

After driving through water, press the brakes gently to dry them. Listen for grinding or a soft pedal.

Leaks and seals

Check door and boot seals, and look for wet carpets or a damp smell. Keep sunroof drain holes clear of leaves.

Rust protection

Wet weather speeds up rust under the car. Ask us about underbody coating.

Important: never start a car that has been in deep water. Starting it can wreck the engine. Call us for towing.
